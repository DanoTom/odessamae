# Letras

Diez capítulos listos para Suno, en orden de publicación. Los impares son baladas (estilo A), los pares medios tiempos (estilo B) y el 9 y el 10 son los inéditos de blues (estilo C); cada canción trae su propio prompt de estilo, ajustado a su tono, tonalidad y tempo. El campo Exclude Styles es el mismo para todas (pestaña principal, en Producción).

| Cap. | Título | Estilo | Qué pasa |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | You Walked Me Home | A | Lonnie la acompaña a casa por el camino largo |
| 2 | Blue Sunday Dress | B | El vestido azul y el primer baile en lo de Mama Rose |
| 3 | Two Cups on the Table | A | La casita; él se va a Chicago y promete mandarla a buscar |
| 4 | Every Letter Says Next Month | B | Las cartas se van achicando hasta que dejan de llegar |
| 5 | Leave the Porch Light On | A | Ella espera con la luz prendida |
| 6 | Mama Says | B | Todos le dicen que lo suelte |
| 7 | First Night at Mama Rose's | A | Canta en público por primera vez |
| 8 | I Don't Live There Anymore | B | Lonnie quiere volver; ella ya es otra |
| 9 | Chicago Took My Man | C | La bronca, en forma de blues |
| 10 | I Turned the Porch Light Off | C | El cierre: apaga la luz sin rencor |

**Revisión de cada letra (10 minutos)**

- [ ] Leerla en voz alta: si una línea no te la creés, marcala y la reescribo.
- [ ] Revisar los nombres inventados (Lonnie, Mama Rose, Miller, Hammond, Mr. Willis) y cambiar los que no te gusten en todas las canciones a la vez.
- [ ] Pegar en Suno solo el texto del bloque, con el estilo que indica la tabla.
- [ ] Si Suno apura alguna línea, acortarla antes de regenerar.

## Acto I: el amor

### 1. You Walked Me Home — estilo A

El camino largo es la primera aparición del motivo que cierra el capítulo 10. El último estribillo cambia una línea y pasa del recuerdo a la confesión.

**Style of Music**

```text
Tender 1962 Southern soul ballad, C major. Warm Hammond organ, clean tremolo guitar, upright bass, brushed drums. Mature female alto up front, shy intimate verses, warm gospel lift on chorus. Analog tape, 68 BPM.
```

**Lyrics**

```text
[Intro | soft organ and guitar, no vocals]

[Verse 1 | intimate, close-mic]
Church let out at half past twelve,
the gravel hot beneath my shoes,
you said you knew a shady way,
I said I had nothing to lose.

[Pre-Chorus | gentle lift]
Past the feed store, past the mill,
you held your hat and kept so still,

[Chorus | full voice, backing singers answer]
you walked me home (the long way round),
three miles of red dirt road,
you walked me home (the long way round),
just so you wouldn't have to go.

[Verse 2 | intimate, a smile in the voice]
My mama watched us through the screen,
she hollered, "Girl, you come inside!"
You tipped your hat and backed away
like a man who'd won a prize.

[Chorus | full voice, backing singers answer]
You walked me home (the long way round),
three miles of red dirt road,
you walked me home (the long way round),
just so you wouldn't have to go.

[Bridge | stripped down, organ and voice only]
Now a straight road, Lonnie,
gets you there before you blink,
but the long way, Lord, the long way
gave my heart the time to think.

[Final Chorus | horns enter, biggest vocal]
You walked me home (the long way round),
I didn't tell you, but I knew,
you walked me home (the long way round),
and every road since runs to you.

[Outro | fade on organ]
The long way round (the long way round).

[End]
```

### 2. Blue Sunday Dress — estilo B

Humor y coqueteo; el vestido vuelve en el capítulo 7. El coro responde como si fuera la gente del salón.

**Style of Music**

```text
Joyful mid-tempo 1962 Memphis soul, F major. Bright horn section, Wurlitzer, walking bass, handclaps on 2 and 4. Mature female alto up front, playful and flirty, call-and-response backing singers. Warm analog tape, 104 BPM.
```

**Lyrics**

```text
[Intro | horn riff, tight groove]

[Verse 1 | playful, light]
I starched the shirts for Mrs. Grey,
saved every nickel in a can,
I bought the bluest dress at Hammond's
to catch the eye of one good man.

[Pre-Chorus | building]
Saturday night at Mama Rose's,
the band was hot, the floor was wood,

[Chorus | call and response with backing singers]
he said, "Is that a Sunday dress?" (Sunday dress)
I said, "It's Saturday, I guess" (Saturday, I guess),
he spun me round till Monday came,
and I ain't never been the same (blue Sunday dress).

[Verse 2 | playful]
He didn't dance like city boys,
he danced like someone fixing fences,
all elbows, careful, kind of slow,
I laughed until I lost my manners.

[Pre-Chorus | building]
Saturday night at Mama Rose's,
the band was hot, the floor was wood,

[Chorus | call and response with backing singers]
he said, "Is that a Sunday dress?" (Sunday dress)
I said, "It's Saturday, I guess" (Saturday, I guess),
he spun me round till Monday came,
and I ain't never been the same (blue Sunday dress).

[Bridge | drums and bass only, sly]
Church folks say that dress is sinful,
church folks ought to see him smile,
I'll wear it Sunday to the service,
let 'em whisper down the aisle.

[Final Chorus | full horns, biggest vocal]
He said, "Is that a Sunday dress?" (Sunday dress)
I said, "It is now, I confess" (I confess),
he spun me round till Monday came,
and I ain't never been the same (blue Sunday dress).

[Outro | horn riff, band stops on the last hit]

[End]
```

### 3. Two Cups on the Table — estilo A

Lo cotidiano y la partida. El estribillo final convierte la costumbre de las dos tazas en una promesa de espera.

**Style of Music**

```text
Warm bittersweet 1963 Southern soul ballad, G major. Hammond organ, clean electric guitar arpeggios, upright bass, brushed drums. Mature female alto up front, tender verses, aching chorus, soft horns at the end. Analog tape, 70 BPM.
```

**Lyrics**

```text
[Intro | soft organ, no vocals]

[Verse 1 | intimate, close-mic]
Two rooms out back of Miller's gin,
a tin roof talking when it rained,
you fixed the hinge, I sewed the curtains,
we wrote our names on the windowpane.

[Pre-Chorus | gentle lift]
Every morning, half past five,
you kissed me 'fore the whistle cried,

[Chorus | full voice, backing singers answer]
two cups on the table (two cups),
one sugar, one black,
two cups on the table (two cups),
and you always coming back.

[Verse 2 | quieter, uneasy]
Then Thursday brought a letter
with a Chicago stamp on top,
your cousin found you work at Swift's,
forty dollars, and the work don't stop.

[Pre-Chorus | gentle lift]
You said, "Baby, just till spring,
then I'll send for you and everything,"

[Chorus | full voice, backing singers answer]
two cups on the table (two cups),
one sugar, one black,
two cups on the table (two cups),
and you always coming back.

[Bridge | stripped down, organ and voice only]
The six-ten northbound took you,
I stood until the smoke was gone,
I walked back to the table,
and your coffee still was warm.

[Final Chorus | horns enter, biggest vocal]
Two cups on the table (two cups),
one sugar, one black,
I'm keeping both cups on the table (two cups)
till you come walking back.

[Outro | fade on organ]

[End]
```

## Acto II: la pérdida

### 4. Every Letter Says Next Month — estilo B

Medio tiempo agridulce: la música sigue alegre mientras las cartas se achican. El perfume de agosto insinúa otra mujer sin decirlo.

**Style of Music**

```text
Bittersweet mid-tempo 1963 Memphis soul, D major. Punchy horns, Wurlitzer, tight bass and drums groove, tambourine. Mature female alto up front, hopeful smile hiding hurt, call-and-response backing singers. Warm analog tape, 96 BPM.
```

**Lyrics**

```text
[Intro | horns, bittersweet groove]

[Verse 1 | light, hopeful]
First letter came in April,
a money order folded twice,
you said the city's big and loud,
the bosses mean, the money nice.

[Pre-Chorus | building]
I read it to the kitchen walls,
I read it to the neighbor's cat,

[Chorus | call and response with backing singers]
every letter says "next month" (next month),
every letter's getting thin (getting thin),
every letter says "next month,"
and every month I let you in.

[Verse 2 | lighter, forcing the smile]
June it said the rent went up,
July it said the job was slow,
August smelled like Evening in Paris,
and I didn't ask, I didn't want to know.

[Pre-Chorus | building]
I read it to the kitchen walls,
I read it to the neighbor's cat,

[Chorus | call and response with backing singers]
every letter says "next month" (next month),
every letter's getting thin (getting thin),
every letter says "next month,"
and every month I let you in.

[Bridge | drums and bass only, voice low]
A man who's coming, Mama told me,
don't need four pages to explain,
I fold your letters into squares,
and I don't read 'em twice again.

[Final Chorus | band drops out on the last line]
September, not a letter (not a letter),
not a word, not a dime (not a dime),
every letter said "next month,"
I guess next month ain't mine.

[End]
```

### 5. Leave the Porch Light On — estilo A

El corazón del canal y el mejor candidato a Short. La línea final cambia el sentido de todo: la luz sigue esperando aunque él ya no venga.

**Style of Music**

```text
Heartbroken 1963 deep Southern soul ballad, A minor. Hammond organ, clean electric guitar, upright bass, brushed drums. Mature female alto up front, intimate verses, gospel-belted chorus, horns on final chorus. Analog tape, 64 BPM.
```

**Lyrics**

```text
[Intro | soft organ and guitar, no vocals]

[Verse 1 | intimate, close-mic]
I kept your coffee cup, still on the sill,
the porch light burning, burning still,
the neighbors whisper, let them talk,
I know the sound of your walk.

[Pre-Chorus | gentle lift]
Every car that slows down on the highway,
every dog that barks at night,

[Chorus | full voice, backing singers answer]
I leave the porch light on (leave it on),
the moths have made a church of it,
the light bill says turn it off,
but, Lord, I just can't quit (can't quit).

[Verse 2 | intimate, a little harder]
Your brother says you found a woman,
your mama says you found the bottle,
I say the city's big, that's all,
a man can lose his way a little.

[Chorus | full voice, backing singers answer]
I leave the porch light on (leave it on),
the moths have made a church of it,
the light bill says turn it off,
but, Lord, I just can't quit (can't quit).

[Bridge | stripped down, organ and voice only]
Forty watts against the darkness,
it ain't much, but it's my own,
so if you're standing in the road tonight,
you'll know somebody's home.

[Final Chorus | horns enter, biggest vocal]
I leave the porch light on (leave it on),
I bought another bulb today,
the light bill says turn it off,
but the light don't know you went away.

[Outro | hummed, fade on organ]
Leave it on (leave it on).

[End]
```

### 6. Mama Says — estilo B

El coro hace de mamá, la hermana y el pastor. El puente es un diálogo y el último estribillo deja que la duda gane.

**Style of Music**

```text
Sassy mid-tempo 1963 gospel-soul stomp, B flat major. Horn stabs, piano, handclaps, tambourine, driving bass. Mature female alto up front, playful and defiant, church-choir backing singers answering every line. Warm analog tape, 108 BPM.
```

**Lyrics**

```text
[Intro | horns and handclaps]

[Verse 1 | playful, sassy]
Mama came by Tuesday morning
with a pie and a piece of her mind,
said, "Girl, you're thinner than a hymnal page,
and that boy ain't worth your time."

[Chorus | call and response, backing singers as the family]
Mama says let him go (let him go),
Sister says let him go (let him go),
Pastor said it Sunday morning (let him go),
but nobody knows him like I know.

[Verse 2 | playful, teasing]
Mr. Willis from the hardware
asked me out to see a show,
he's got a Buick and his own front teeth,
and he laughs a little slow.

[Chorus | call and response, backing singers as the family]
Mama says let him go (let him go),
Sister says let him go (let him go),
Pastor said it Sunday morning (let him go),
but nobody knows him like I know.

[Bridge | drums only, spoken-sung dialogue]
I said, "Mama, what if he's coming?"
("Baby, what if he ain't?")
I said, "Mama, love is waiting,"
("Honey, no, it ain't.")

[Final Chorus | full band, biggest vocal]
Mama says let him go (let him go),
Sister says let him go (let him go),
even the porch light's flickering (let him go),
maybe everybody knows.

[End]
```

## Acto III: la reconstrucción

### 7. First Night at Mama Rose's — estilo A

Vuelve el vestido azul. Una sola palabra cambia en el último estribillo: de "for you" a "for me".

**Style of Music**

```text
Smoky 1963 nightclub soul ballad, E flat major. Piano, Hammond organ, upright bass, brushed drums, soft room ambience. Mature female alto up front, nervous intimate start, growing confidence, gospel peak on final chorus. Analog tape, 66 BPM.
```

**Lyrics**

```text
[Intro | soft organ, a room murmuring, no vocals]

[Verse 1 | intimate, nervous]
Rose said, "Sugar, you can sing,
I hear you through the kitchen door,"
she put me on at half past ten
between the drunks and the dance floor.

[Pre-Chorus | gentle lift]
I wore the blue dress, it still fit,
I took a breath and held on to it,

[Chorus | full voice, backing singers answer]
I sang it for you, Lonnie (for you),
every word I couldn't write,
I sang it for you, Lonnie (for you),
the first time, the first night.

[Verse 2 | growing confidence]
A man up front stopped talking,
a woman set down her glass,
somebody hollered, "Sing it, sister,"
and I felt something give at last.

[Chorus | full voice, backing singers answer]
I sang it for you, Lonnie (for you),
every word I couldn't write,
I sang it for you, Lonnie (for you),
the first time, the first night.

[Bridge | stripped down, organ and voice only]
Somewhere in the second chorus
I stopped looking at the door,
I wasn't calling for you, Lonnie,
I was singing, nothing more.

[Final Chorus | horns enter, biggest vocal]
I sang it for me, Lonnie (for me),
every word I couldn't write,
I sang it for me, Lonnie (for me),
the first time, the first night.

[Outro | applause fades into organ]

[End]
```

I sing on Fridays down at Rose's,

Well, you can come on down to Miller's gin,

### 9. Chicago Took My Man — estilo C (inédito)

Blues clásico en estrofas AAB, con la ciudad como rival. Retoma la cuenta de la luz del capítulo 5.

**Style of Music**

```text
Slow 1950s Chicago-style blues, 12-bar, E flat. Piano triplets, muted trumpet, upright bass, brushed drums. Weary female alto up front, conversational with a sharp edge, sustained chest voice at peaks. Late-night club room, 58 BPM.
```

**Lyrics**

```text
[Intro | slow piano triplets, muted trumpet]

[Verse 1 | weary, conversational]
Chicago, you're a greedy town,
Lord, Chicago, you're a greedy town,
you had a million men, you took the one I found.

[Verse 2 | weary, a little sharper]
You paid him on a Friday, put whiskey in his hand,
you paid him on a Friday, put whiskey in his hand,
by Monday he forgot he was a Mississippi man.

[Instrumental | muted trumpet solo over piano]

[Verse 3 | sustained chest voice]
I'm done crying, crying won't pay the light bill,
I'm done crying, crying won't pay the light bill,
I'll sing this on a Friday, let Chicago foot the bill.

[Verse 4 | quiet, final]
So keep him, Chicago, keep him warm and fed,
keep him, Chicago, keep him warm and fed,
the man I loved is gone, you got somebody else instead.

[Outro | piano alone]

[End]
```

### 10. I Turned the Porch Light Off — estilo C (inédito)

El cierre de la compilación. El puente cita el capítulo 1 y el último estribillo resuelve la espera sin drama.

**Style of Music**

```text
Tender 1950s slow blues ballad, F major. Piano, upright bass, muted trumpet answering the vocal. Warm, weary female alto up front, forgiving, quiet resolve, sustained final notes. Late-night room, vinyl warmth, 60 BPM.
```

**Lyrics**

```text
[Intro | slow piano, upright bass]

[Verse 1 | weary, tender]
Went back to Miller's gin today,
the roof is rust, the screen is torn,
somebody's babies on the step,
a new name painted on the door.

[Chorus | sustained, backing singers soft]
I turned the porch light off, Lonnie (turned it off),
I didn't slam the door,
I just turned the porch light off, Lonnie,
I don't need it anymore.

[Verse 2 | conversational]
I don't hate you, never could,
you were young and scared and poor,
and I was young and thought that love
was waiting by a door.

[Chorus | sustained, backing singers soft]
I turned the porch light off, Lonnie (turned it off),
I didn't slam the door,
I just turned the porch light off, Lonnie,
I don't need it anymore.

[Bridge | piano and voice only]
Every song I ever sang
started on that red dirt road,
you walked me home the long way round,
and now I walk it on my own.

[Final Chorus | muted trumpet answers, biggest vocal]
I turned the porch light off, Lonnie (turned it off),
and the whole sky lit the yard,
I turned the porch light off, Lonnie,
and it wasn't even hard.

[Outro | piano alone, slow fade]

[End]
```
